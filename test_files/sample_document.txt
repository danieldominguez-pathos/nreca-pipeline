NRECA Document: Rural Electric Cooperative Guidelines

Introduction to Rural Electric Cooperatives

The National Rural Electric Cooperative Association (NRECA) represents America's electric cooperatives.
Electric cooperatives are private, independent electric utilities owned by the members they serve.
More than 900 electric cooperatives serve 42 million people in 47 states.

Key Principles of Electric Cooperatives:

1. Open and Voluntary Membership
   Electric cooperatives are open to all persons who can reasonably use their services.

2. Democratic Member Control
   Electric cooperatives are democratic organizations controlled by their members.
   Members have equal voting rights (one member, one vote).

3. Members' Economic Participation
   Members contribute equitably to, and democratically control, the capital of their cooperative.

4. Autonomy and Independence
   Electric cooperatives are autonomous, self-help organizations controlled by their members.

5. Education, Training, and Information
   Electric cooperatives provide education and training for their members and employees.

6. Cooperation Among Cooperatives
   Electric cooperatives serve their members most effectively by working together.

7. Concern for Community
   Cooperatives work for the sustainable development of their communities.

Benefits of Electric Cooperatives:

- Local ownership and control
- Not-for-profit operation
- Focus on member service
- Community investment
- Reliable electricity delivery

For more information, contact your local electric cooperative or visit NRECA.coop.
